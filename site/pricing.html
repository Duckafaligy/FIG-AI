You pay per site, not per person

An agency with three logins and four hundred client sites pays for four hundred, not three. Charging per seat would mean a three-person agency auditing four hundred sites for the price of three logins — not a discount, a broken meter, and one that would stop the product surviving its best customers.

The free read

Anyone can read any public site, without an account, a few times a day per browser. No ownership proof required — studying a site you admire to work out why it holds together is how most people learn, and locking that behind a login would kill the most useful thing here. Run one now →

Questions and answers

What counts as a site?

One hostname. example.com and shop.example.com are two. Pages within a site are not charged separately.

What happens after the free week?

Nothing automatic. There is no card on file, so it cannot start charging you. Your reads stay visible and you decide whether to subscribe.

Can I change how many sites I have?

Any time, in either direction. Adding sites is billed pro rata from the next invoice; removing them stops the charge. An annual commitment can set a floor if you would rather lock a rate.

Is the rate volume or graduated?

Volume. At 30 sites every site costs the 30-site rate, rather than the first four costing more. The Stripe price is configured the same way, so the invoice matches the table above.

Do you charge per page?

No. An audit covers up to 40 pages of a site and that is included. Very large estates — catalogues in the thousands of pages — are a conversation rather than a checkbox; say hello.

Is there a free plan?

There is a free read, permanently, for anyone — no account needed. There is no free tier of the dashboard beyond the trial week, because a scheduled crawler that costs money on a schedule cannot be free forever without becoming somebody else’s bill.

Can I cancel?

Whenever you like, from the billing page. You keep access until the end of the period you have paid for.

Will you build a way to check somebody else’s work for AI use?

No, and not ever. FIG reports patterns on sites you choose to read; it does not judge authorship, because nothing reliably can. Why that matters.
